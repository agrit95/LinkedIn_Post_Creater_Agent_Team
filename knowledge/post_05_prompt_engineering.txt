Prompt engineering is dead.

Before you come at me — hear me out.

Six months ago, writing the perfect prompt was a superpower. People were selling $500 courses on "10x your ChatGPT output with these 50 prompt templates."

Today? AI models understand context so well that a simple, clear instruction beats a fancy prompt template 9 times out of 10.

What actually matters now:

1. Knowing WHAT to ask (domain expertise)
2. Knowing WHEN to use AI vs doing it yourself (judgment)
3. Knowing HOW to build systems around AI (agent design)

The prompt is the least important part of the chain.

I've seen junior developers write simple prompts and get better results than "prompt engineers" with elaborate templates. Why? Because the junior dev understood the actual problem they were solving.

The real skill isn't writing prompts. It's understanding your problem deeply enough that any prompt works.

Stop perfecting your prompts. Start perfecting your understanding of the problem.

The AI will figure out the rest.

Controversial? Maybe. But I've built 15 AI-powered tools this year and not once did a "prompt template" make the difference. Understanding the use case did.

Agree or disagree?
