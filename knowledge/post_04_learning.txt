I learned more in 30 days of building projects than in 2 years of watching tutorials.

This isn't motivational fluff. It's a pattern I keep seeing.

Here's what happens when you only consume content:
- You feel productive (you're not)
- You understand concepts in theory (you can't apply them)
- You collect bookmarks (you never revisit them)
- You stay comfortable (you never get stuck)

Here's what happens when you build:
- You hit errors you've never seen before (and learn to fix them)
- You make architecture decisions (and learn from bad ones)
- You ship something real (and get feedback from real users)
- You build confidence (because you have proof, not just knowledge)

The tutorial trap is real. I was stuck in it for years.

What broke me out? A simple rule I still follow:

For every 1 hour of learning, spend 3 hours building.

Not 1:1. Not "build when you feel ready." 3x more building than learning. Always.

You'll feel uncomfortable. That's the point.

The discomfort is the learning. The tutorials are just the warm-up.

What's something you learned way faster by building than by studying?
